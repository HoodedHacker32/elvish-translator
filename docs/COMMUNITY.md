# What the Elvish community says, checked against Eldamo

Notes from reading what people who study Tolkien's languages say, so the translator matches current
understanding. Every change below was checked in Eldamo (version 0.8.13, which includes Parma
Eldalamberon 23) before it was made. Where the community and Eldamo differ, Eldamo wins, and the
difference is written down here.

Last checked: November 2026.

## Sources read

- r/Sindarin (reddit.com/r/sindarin): the pinned FAQ on permanent inscriptions, and threads on
  "What's the best Sindarin translator?", "How to say thank you", "Help with where to start",
  "Collecting resources to learn Sindarin", "Should there be a Duolingo course for Sindarin?", and a
  machine-translated text people were asked to check.
- Ryszard Derdziński's Sindarin Ring-verse (Gwaith-i-Phethdain, elvish.org/gwaith/ringpoem.htm,
  revised 2003), with his notes on each word.

## Changed

| Point | What the community says | Eldamo | Done |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sindarin "we" | Tolkien's latest ending is -(o)f, not the -m everyone used for decades | 1969 -f (PE22/167): athof "we will"; paradigm cerif, galof, carathof | Present and future use -f. Tolkien's own penim "we lack" (PE17/144) is kept |
| "all" | Derdziński reads the King's Letter phain as the plural of pân | pân "all", only as phain in mhellyn în phain "all his friends" (SD/129) | Plural pain after the noun phrase: mellyn nín pain, hain … pain |
| "thank you" | No attested verb. The films' hannon le predates the published root and its h- cannot be right; Neo-Sindarin writers say annon allen, which also reads "I give to you" | anna- "give", also used for "thank" in Eldamo | Both offered, both marked fan-made, with that explanation |
| "bring", "bind" | Derdziński uses toged (from Noldorin tog-) and nuded (from Noldorin nud-) | tog- and gwedh- stand; nud- "*tie" is deprecated in favour of nod- | tog- and gwedh-, marked as Tolkien's earlier drafts |
| Tattoos and engravings | The FAQ advises English written in Tengwar, not an Elvish translation, for anything permanent | (not a language question) | Said on the print sheet |
| Machine translators | "None of them really works", and AI tools mix up Quenya and Sindarin | (not a language question) | The translator already rates every line and withholds the weak ones |

## Deliberately not changed

- **The article.** Late in life Tolkien seems to have turned the Sindarin article i into en, which
  clashes with almost everything in The Lord of the Rings. Eldamo still gives i for the article, and so
  does every line of Tolkien's that visitors know, so i stays.
- **"where".** Derdziński and earlier translators use ias, built from Quenya yassë. It is not in Eldamo
  and not Tolkien's, so the translator offers his ennas "there" instead ("…, ennas …").
- **"their".** Derdziński uses în, reading the King's Letter word as a reflexive "own" for any person.
  Eldamo glosses it as "his (own)" only, so "their" is not translated; "the" is offered instead.
- **"we" in the past tense.** Eldamo gives no 1969 past-tense "we", so the older -em stays (savannem).
- **His inventions.** orchamm "throne" and Ellerain "Elven-kings" are Derdziński's own. Tolkien's
  arahadhw "throne" (PE22) is used instead.

## Worth knowing

- Learners on r/Sindarin are pointed to Eldamo, Ardalambion, the Parf Edhellen dictionary and the
  Vinyë Lambengolmor Discord server. "A Gateway to Sindarin" is called outdated and teaches the films'
  Sindarin, and one popular beginner site is said to be full of inaccuracies.
- **Name:** Thorsten Renk's well-known Sindarin course is called *Pedin Edhellen*. The learning app's
  name, *Pedo Edhellen*, is one letter away.
